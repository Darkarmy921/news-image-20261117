# NEWS.md — sources for the stories in the v2 image

Every claim in the image was checked against reporting dated Tuesday
17 November 2026 (or first reported that day). Figures are only those
the cited reporting gives. Nothing is invented.

## 1. Kerch Bridge road arch collapses — LEAD

Ukrainian strikes hit the 19-km Kerch Bridge (road + rail link, Russia
to occupied Crimea) for a second straight day; the road arch and a road
span collapsed into the Kerch Strait on the morning of 17 Nov. The first
strike (Mon 16 Nov) damaged the arch; the second strike early Tue
finished it. Traffic was suspended on road and rail; the Kremlin vowed
to rebuild; the railway arch still stood, and BBC reported some rail
services later resumed.

Verified by Reuters (verified video: the main road arch "crumbled into
the sea"), BBC / BBC Verify (geolocated footage), CNN, the Guardian,
and the Kyiv Independent. Russia's Transport Ministry confirmed the
suspension. No span-length figure is used in the image (secondary
sources disagree: 227 m vs 273 m), and the claimed weapon type is
unverified and not used.

## 2. U.S. and Iran trade fire in the Gulf

Iran's Revolutionary Guards launched drones at a U.S. military radar at
Sheikh Isa Air Base, Bahrain, on 17 Nov — the 3rd Iranian attack on U.S.
targets in Bahrain in 5 days. Bahrain and U.S. Central Command: the
drones were intercepted, no damage to U.S. equipment, no injuries. Hours
later U.S. fighter jets struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm
Island (Strait of Hormuz) with precision munitions, described by a U.S.
military official as self-defence.

Verified by Reuters, AP, CBS, Al Jazeera, and AFP. Casualty and damage
claims are each side's own and are not asserted beyond "no injuries /
damage reported" per CENTCOM/Bahrain.

Related, same theatre: the IRGC said on Mon that two tankers struck
naval mines near the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire, and a UKMTO
report on Tue spoke of a second strike on a disabled tanker near
Khasab, Oman. Iran's mine claims could not be independently verified
(AP, Reuters, Al Jazeera), so the image carries only the careful line:
"Tankers were also hit near Hormuz; Iran's mine claims are unverified."

## 3. Trump lands in Manila; first Quad summit in two years

President Donald Trump arrived in Manila on Tue 17 Nov, first stop of a
three-day Asia trip. The same day he met the Quad leaders (U.S., Japan,
India, Australia) — the first Quad leaders' meeting in two years (since
September 2024) and the first of Trump's second term. The Quad announced
an AI accelerator and critical-minerals agreements; the joint statement
voiced concern over economic coercion without naming China. Trump said
Thailand will eliminate all tariffs on U.S. exports.

Verified by AP, Reuters, and AFP.

## 4. Nvidia sales more than double

Nvidia reported Q3 revenue of $115.8B, up 103% year on year
(data-centre revenue $107.6B, +110%), beating about $109.1B expected;
diluted EPS $2.70. It guided Q4 revenue to $130B (±3%), above about
$125B expected. These were the first results since CEO Jensen Huang
resigned (12 Nov); the board ruled out his return and launched a CEO
search.

Verified by the company report as covered by Motley Fool and
Reuters-derived briefs, 17 Nov after the bell.

## 5. Chase software bug slows U.S. banking

A software bug in JPMorgan Chase's on-premise hardware systems slowed
online banking, branch transactions and Zelle payments on Mon 16 Nov,
with knock-on delays at Bank of America, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank,
Capital One and Truist. Downdetector recorded about 4,800 Chase reports
at the ~2pm ET peak. The bank said there was "no evidence this is
cyber-related" and "not a systemic issue"; customers kept access to
accounts and funds, and transactions were processing normally again by
Mon evening, confirmed Tue.

Verified by ABC News, CNBC, AP, Bloomberg, Euronews, and CBS.
